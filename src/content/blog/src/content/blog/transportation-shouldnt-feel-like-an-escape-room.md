Last weekend, I did an escape room.

The goal was simple: find clues, solve puzzles, and eventually figure out how to get out. It was fun because it was a game.

Unfortunately, too many shipping departments play the same game every day — except it isn't optional.

Instead of solving riddles, they're trying to answer questions that should have simple answers:

- Where's the truck?
- Has anyone actually talked to the driver?
- Is that ETA real, or is it another guess?
- Is the shipment still on schedule?
- Should I warn my customer?

One unanswered question becomes five. Production starts asking for updates, customer service needs answers, and sales wants to know if a delivery will be late. Before long, your team is spending valuable time investigating a shipment instead of doing the work they were hired to do.

The frustrating part is that most of these situations aren't caused by a late truck.

They're caused by a lack of communication.

Customers are surprisingly understanding when they're told the truth. Traffic happens. Breakdowns happen. Weather happens. Delays are part of transportation.

What frustrates people is uncertainty.

Not knowing whether the driver has been reached. Not knowing whether the ETA is accurate. Not knowing whether anyone is actually managing the problem.

That's where transportation providers earn — or lose — trust.

Good logistics isn't just about getting freight from Point A to Point B. It's about owning the shipment, communicating proactively, and making sure your customer never has to become the dispatcher just to find out what's happening.

A shipment shouldn't require detective work.

Transportation shouldn't feel like an escape room.
